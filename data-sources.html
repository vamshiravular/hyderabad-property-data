Map geometry and the expanded footprint

The homepage contains all 300 wards, 60 circles, and 12 zones in the 2026 boundary dataset published by Hyderabad Urban Lab / Hyderabad Urban Observatory’s Accessibility Atlas. This includes newly merged peripheral municipal areas; it does not use only the old 150-ward GHMC footprint. The source includes Tellapur, Ameenpur, Patancheruvu, Medchal, Shamirpet, Keesara, Ghatkesar, Badangpet, Adibatla, and Shamshabad.

The source’s dataset manifest is dated 12 July 2026. Raven’s display geometries were reconstructed from the source’s published zoom-14 vector tiles, clipped at tile edges, dissolved by feature identity, simplified, and rounded for web use. They are digitised representations and can contain source or conversion errors. This is not a cadastral survey or certified legal boundary layer. Verify the relevant government notification before relying on a jurisdiction.

Secunderabad Cantonment Board, BHEL Township, and the airport are shown as separate grey areas. Their depiction is a mapping convention from the source, not a claim that there are four municipal corporations. We preserve source ward, circle, and zone names; a familiar locality name need not describe exactly the same area as a ward.

Government value and market value

No verified rupee values have yet been imported into the map. Government registration values vary by village, street, classification, property type, and effective date. An apartment rate per square foot cannot be compared directly with a land rate per square yard. Use the official Telangana Registration & Stamps Market Value Search for the exact property.

A market asking price is a seller’s request. A completed transaction price is a different observation. A future market range will state which measure it represents, the property type, units, observation dates, sample size, deduplication approach, and source. Small or unverified samples will not produce a range. A ward-wide rate is not an appraisal of an individual property.

The original area-guide pages retain explicitly fictional examples for demonstration. They are excluded from the homepage map. Read the methodology and calculator for definitions.

Messages and community comments

The Talk to us form collects your name, reply email, area, query, and consent. When connected, the server forwards it to the owner’s private inbox using the configured mail service. The receiving inbox is stored only in server settings. The copy-query option copies text locally; it does not send a message. A successful API response means the mail provider accepted the message for delivery, not that the recipient has read it.

Community posts and replies are stored in the configured shared database, not browser storage. Names and comments are public; email enquiries are private. No login is currently required, so display names are unverified and can be impersonated. Do not share confidential documents, personal contact details, or private addresses in public comments. The owner can hide comments through the database’s moderation status. Server-side validation and hourly rate limits apply. A keyed one-way hash is used for abuse controls; raw visitor IP addresses are not stored in the application tables. Hosting and service providers may retain their own operational logs.

The interface displays connection availability and does not pretend to send messages or publish comments while services are disconnected. To request removal or report abuse, use Talk to us.
